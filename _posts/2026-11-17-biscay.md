## Key Idea

Cellular bandwidth swings so fast that end-to-end congestion control is always guessing a step behind. Biscay stops guessing. It reads radio KPIs straight from the phone's modem, like the resource blocks the tower granted, and turns them into the link's real capacity using the same 3GPP tables the modem uses. A kernel module called OpenDiag pulls these KPIs about every 11 ms instead of every second. Next to BBR, Biscay cuts average delay by about 58% at the same throughput.

## Critique

The bandwidth estimate is impressive, with a correlation above 0.95 to real throughput. But "practical" is doing a lot of work. It only runs on Qualcomm modems, and installing OpenDiag takes a custom Android image, so skipping root isn't the same as being easy to ship. I also wish the wired-bottleneck fallback was tested in the wild and not just with throttling they set up themselves.

## Connections

BBR estimates bottleneck bandwidth by watching delivery rate and probing for more. Biscay keeps BBR's cwnd formula (bandwidth times RTT) and just swaps in a direct reading from the radio. When the cellular link isn't the bottleneck, it falls back to plain BBR. It's BBR's model with a better sensor.
