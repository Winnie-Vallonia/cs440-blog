## Key Idea

dcSim targets the packet loss that cripples AI training collectives on cheap, lossy datacenter switches. Its fix is to send a tiny Sim probe down a random path first, and only send the real Data packet once the Sim proves that path is clear, turning congestion avoidance into a pre-check instead of a recovery problem.

## Critique

The honesty is what sold me: Section 3.1 proves their own guarantee can fail in multi-switch networks and settles for "practically," not deterministically, lossless. But the fix, a 2x Data-to-Sim buffer ratio, is only validated on one fat-tree testbed, and the paper never breaks out probe overhead as its own cost once you scale to many concurrent collectives.

## Connections

This reads like DCTCP's problem, tiny commodity buffers, reheated for AI training traffic fifteen years later, except the fix changes philosophy rather than parameters: DCTCP reacts more gracefully, dcSim refuses to react to real data at all. It also leans on the same per-flow ECMP hashing trick from [Unlocking ECMP Programmability]({% post_url 2026-10-08-unlocking-ecmp-programmability %}), just repurposed to randomize probe paths instead of application flows
