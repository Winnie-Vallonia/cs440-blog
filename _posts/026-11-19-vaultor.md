## Key Idea

Tor hidden services hide their IP, but the server has to stay online to answer every request, and that uptime is what attacks like guard discovery and clock skew feed on. VaulTor moves the service off the provider's machine. A volunteer "vault" runs the provider's code inside an Intel SGX enclave and serves clients, so the provider only comes online to upload or update content. The private key never leaves the enclave, so even the vault owner can't read it. On the real Tor network, pages loaded at most about 6% slower than a normal hidden service.

## Critique

Cutting uptime is smart since it weakens several attacks at once. But the whole design leans on SGX, and side-channel attacks are declared out of scope, a lot to skip for SGX. The attack defenses are argued, not tested, and the evaluation is one laptop with no load testing.

## Connections

The 2004 Tor paper lists "smear-resistance" as a hidden service goal, so a relay can't get framed for hosting something illegal. VaulTor flips that. The vault really is hosting someone else's content, and the paper admits operators may face legal risk. Tor protected the middleman. VaulTor asks the middleman to take the hit.
