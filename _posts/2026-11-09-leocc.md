## Key Idea

Congestion control usually treats loss and delay spikes as a sign the network is jammed. On Starlink, that's wrong a lot of the time.
Satellites move fast, so every 15 seconds the network reshuffles which satellite talks to your dish. Each reshuffle can suddenly change the capacity, the RTT, and the loss.
What surprised me is how LeoCC catches this. It pings the ground side every 10 ms and watches for a gap of about 45 ms between replies. That gap means a reshuffle just happened, so it throws away old measurements and re-estimates the link.
It gets 85 to 494% more throughput than Cubic, Copa, and BBRv3.

## Critique

I wish they tested more than Starlink. There are only three dishes, all residential, and the 45 ms threshold was tuned by hand.
The design also leans on a stable ground-side address to ping, and on Starlink handling ping traffic separately from data. If Starlink changes how it schedules, the detector could stop working.
I also thought it was a bit convenient to say Cubic and Reno look unfair only because they underuse the link.

## Connections

This reminded me of Michel et al., who saw around 1.5 to 2% loss under load on Starlink and pointed out that Cubic treats every loss as congestion.
LeoCC feels like the answer to that. If the loss isn't congestion, backing off just wastes the link.
